I think that same instinct applies here too: being able to reason about what a model's output should look like, where it's likely to fail, and how to judge whether it's good, even before I can build the model myself.

I see this role as a chance to combine that evaluative sense with actual hands-on coding ability, which I'm committed to picking up quickly with some guidance — especially on the ML and Deep Learning fundamentals, where I know I have the most ground to cover.
